# Engagement design — evidence reviewed 17 October 2026

The objective is enjoyable, voluntary repeat play. No claim is made that this game is
"maximally addictive", or that all psychological literature has been incorporated.
There has not yet been a human playtest or retention experiment on this revision.

## Evidence and design choices

Research based on self-determination theory connects game enjoyment and engagement to
competence, autonomy and relatedness. The design choices below are applications of those
ideas, not experimentally established effects in LanBall. See the primary theoretical
paper, [Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan (2010)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2010_PrzybylskiRigbyRyan_ROGP.pdf).

| Design goal | Implemented choice |
|---|---|
| Mastery and understandable feedback | Faster input transport, interpolation, player blocking, consistent kick cooldown, clear scores, goal feedback and practice |
| Player choice | Casual/ranked, selected teams, configurable rounds/pitches, optional sound, key rebinding, reduced motion, text size and high contrast |
| Shared competition | Team chat, Elo-based balancing, head-to-head history and four-team tournaments |
| Meaningful progress | Saved results and rating history, with explanations of what counts; no invented rewards for time spent |
| Clear session boundaries | A saved result after each round; readiness required for a rematch; explicit leave controls and a break-friendly result message |

The longitudinal study [Vuorre et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220411) used
objective play data from 38,935 players and seven games. It cautions against equating
time played with well-being and emphasizes player motivations. That is why this design
does not treat longer sessions as the sole measure of success.

The review [Tyack & Mekler (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12639) examined 259 SDT-based
HCI games papers and criticized shallow applications of theory. Therefore, the choices
above are framed as testable hypotheses, not guarantees or a universal psychological recipe.

A search also surfaced 2026 research on sustained gaming motivation, but the full text
was unavailable in this session. It was not used to justify a specific mechanic. This is
a focused evidence review, not an exhaustive survey through the date in the heading.

## What to test with people

Run short sessions with consenting first-time and returning players on the actual LAN.
Measure perceived responsiveness, understanding of readiness/scoring, competitive fairness,
enjoyment, willingness to replay, and whether players can comfortably stop after a match.
Ask what they found frustrating before adding reward mechanics. Compare smoothing enabled
and disabled and gather feedback from both keyboard and phone players.

No paid/random rewards, loss-of-streak penalties, deceptive scarcity or automatic rematch
loop was added. The game does not collect behavioral analytics or claim a clinical benefit.
